## Executive Summary

Rosewood Hotel Group is a Hong Kong-headquartered ultra-luxury hotel owner-operator, privately held by Chow Tai Fook Enterprises, running Rosewood Hotels & Resorts, New World Hotels & Resorts, Asaya, Carlyle & Co. and Rosewood Residences across 23 countries. What there *is*, is a freshly rebuilt commerce layer (Adobe Experience Manager Cloud + commercetools + Sabre SynXis) with an explicit, unused-looking payment seam at `cart/v1/payment/initiate`, zero orchestrator and zero PSP SDK anywhere in the front end, and at least four or five genuinely separate payment stacks already running across gift cards, two Shopify shops, dining and spa. The motion is **greenfield** — but the honest framing is that the greenfield is the *central capture layer that does not yet exist*, and the first call has to establish whether the group intends to build one.

### Why the two domains disagree — and why it matters for scoring

The domains serve **different audiences, and only one of them can pay.**

- `rosewoodhotels.com` has a checkout. `rosewoodhotelgroup.com` **has none** — it is a Prismic-backed single-page corporate site whose only outbound links are to `careers.rosewoodhotelgroup.com`, `subscription.rosewoodhotelgroup.com` and `picasso.rosewoodhotelgroup.com` (the DAM). Verified by direct fetch: the served HTML is a 6.5 KB JavaScript shell with a single `<title>` and no content, and its Prismic API returns 401.
- **Saudi Arabia at 11.41% on the corporate domain is an owner/developer/careers audience, not guests.** The group has four Saudi projects — Rosewood Jeddah (live), Rosewood AMAALA (opened Aug 2026), Rosewood Red Sea / Shura Island (future), and a Riyadh property — which is exactly the profile that drives development and recruitment traffic to a corporate site. Saudi Arabia does not appear in the booking domain's top 10 at all. **It is EMEA and out of territory regardless.**
- **India at 5.43% on the corporate domain and absent from the booking domain** points the same way: Rosewood has **no property in India** in the live catalog. ### 3C. 🔑 **Where the card actually settles — central vs per-property**

**This is the finding that decides whether the account is real, so it is sourced line by line.**

**Answer: per property. The brand.com booking engine takes a card *guarantee*; the hotel charges the card, in its own currency, under policies the property authors itself.**

**Evidence 1 — the standard rate at the flagship APAC property takes no money.** Live rate API for Rosewood Hong Kong returns exactly one rate plan, `BAR` "Flexible Rate", with:
- booking policy `STD`: **"All reservation must be guaranteed with a credit card."**
- cancel policy `7D`: "Cancel by 6pm local hotel time 7 days prior to arrival to avoid penalty of 1 night stay room charge plus 10% service charge."
No deposit field, no prepayment field, no amount due at booking.
Source: [`/api/catalog/v1/room?hotelId=hong-kong&…`](https://www.rosewoodhotels.com/api/catalog/v1/room?hotelId=hong-kong&checkInDate=2026-12-01&checkOutDate=2026-12-03&numRooms=1&adults=2&currency=HKD)

**Evidence 2 — The Carlyle says it outright.** Booking policy `STD` at The Carlyle, New York: **"Reservations guarantee with credit card. Deposit is not required."**

**Evidence 3 — policy codes are authored per property, with no group standard.** Twelve properties sampled in one pass returned **fourteen different booking-policy codes**: `STD`, `CC`, `GCC`, `CARD`, `DEP`, `VDEP`, `FULL`, `Full`, `FPP`, `SP1`, `500USD`, `48D-FULL`, `NOR`, `NON`. The same intent is spelled differently at every hotel — "All reservation must be guaranteed with a credit card" (Hong Kong), "A valid credit card is required at the time of booking" (Beijing), "Valid credit card guarantee at the time of booking" (Luang Prabang), "Valid credit card required to guarantee reservation at time of booking" (Phuket), "Credit card required at time of booking" (Phnom Penh). That is the signature of per-property configuration in a CRS, not a central payment policy.

**Evidence 4 — the smoking gun. Even the *prepaid* rates are collected offline, by a human.** Rosewood London booking policy `FPP`: **"Full prepayment taken at the time of the booking. Our Reservations Team will contact you within the next 24 hours to arrange the prepayment."** A central online checkout capable of taking card-not-present money would not need a phone call. This single policy string is the clearest evidence in the entire run that **brand.com cannot currently capture.**

**Evidence 5 — each property quotes and settles in its own local currency.** The rate API returns a nested price object where `original` is the property's own currency and the outer object is a display conversion. Across 12 sampled properties: **HKD** (Hong Kong), **THB** (Bangkok, Phuket), **CNY** (Beijing, Guangzhou), **NZD** (Kauri Cliffs), **GBP** (London), **JPY** (Miyakojima), **SAR** (AMAALA), **USD** (The Carlyle, Phnom Penh, Luang Prabang). The booking engine offers **17 display currencies** (USD, CAD, MXN, EUR, BRL, GBP, NZD, THB, CNY, HKD, JPY, AUD, SGD, INR, AED, SAR, QAR) over those local settlement currencies, with `defaultCurrency: "HKD"` at the Hong Kong property.

**Evidence 6 — a property charges its own card surcharge.** Rosewood Kauri Cliffs (New Zealand) booking policy `DEP`: "A one-night deposit including tax is required to confirm a reservation, and a valid credit card must be held on file to maintain a secured reservation. **A 2.6% convenience fee will be applied for all credit card payments.**" A property-level card surcharge is only possible where the property owns the merchant relationship. Its cancellation policy quotes penalties in **NZD250**.

**Evidence 7 — Japan takes a pre-authorisation, not a charge.** Rosewood Miyakojima policy `SP1`: "A valid credit card is required at the time of booking and **a pre-authorization of 100% of the full stay is taken at the time of cancellation due.**"

**Evidence 8 — card data lives in Sabre's systems, not Rosewood's.** The 2017 disclosure states the exposed data sat on Sabre's systems and that *"the issue did not affect Rosewood's own systems"* ([hotel-online](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/security-issue-at-sabre-affects-rosewood-hotel-group-guests-payment-card-in)).

### ⚠️ The critical caveat on every "not found" above — read this before using any of it

**An absence from the merchant's own bundle is not proof of absence, and this report does not treat it as one.** The booking checkout calls `cart/v1/payment/initiate`, which returns 403 without an authenticated cart session. If that endpoint returns a hosted redirect, the method list could be rendered entirely server-side or by a provider's own dashboard, and nothing in the bundle would show it. **This repo has already published one false claim of exactly this shape — asserting a merchant had no Pix in Brazil on the strength of a bundle grep, when the Pix constants were there all along. Brazil is 4.41% of this prospect's booking traffic, so that lesson applies directly here.**

What *can* be said with confidence, because it does not depend on that inference:
1. The seven-brand card map **is** the merchant's own code, rendered by the merchant, with no orchestrator in the path to override it — so cards are definitely the primary presentation.
2. `permissions-policy: payment=()` is a **server-sent response header** that blocks the browser Payment Request API regardless of who renders the method list. Browser-native Apple Pay and Google Pay are genuinely unavailable. A redirect-based Alipay would still be possible.
3. **The strongest and safest formulation of the Hong Kong gap is a comparison, not an absence claim:** the group's own Hong Kong store takes AliPay, AliPay HK, WeChat Pay, PayMe and UnionPay on a sub-US$100 candle, while the booking engine for a US$1,262 stay presents a card form. Both are sourced, both are first-party, and the contrast is the question — *"you already take PayMe and AliPay HK on the shop; what made rooms different?"* — rather than an assertion Prateek would have to defend.

**This is why the "local rail gap" ICP row was scored 0 rather than +3.** The row requires a source for the absence. What exists is strong circumstantial evidence plus an excellent question, and that is worth more as a question than as an overstated point.
