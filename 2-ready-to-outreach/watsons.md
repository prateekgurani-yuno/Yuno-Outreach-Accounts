### Section 10: Strategic Insights & Outreach Angles

> **Insight #1: One platform, eight markets, nine payment stacks, no routing layer visible**
> **Evidence:** Section 3A (DNS: all eight ccTLDs on `prod.aswatson.com.edgekey.net`, all eight APIs on `prod3`) + Section 4 (nine markets, nine distinct local method sets, essentially no shared rail beyond cards).
> **Pain Point:** Adding or changing a rail in any market is a change against a shared platform that serves seven other markets. That makes every local payment decision a central engineering decision, which is precisely how method sets drift apart and why a market ends up without the rail it needs.
> **Yuno Value Proposition:** One integration above the existing platform, per-market method and routing configuration without a per-market platform change.
> **Best Success Case:** **McDonald's / Arcos Dorados** — unified processing across 21 countries is the closest analogue to one platform serving many markets. No public link, so use it conversationally; use **Rappi** where a linkable case is needed.
> **Outreach Angle:** Their eight country storefronts resolve to one origin and one API, yet the payment method set differs in every market. Observation only — both halves are their own.
> **Suggested Subject Line:** *One platform, eight payment stacks*

> **Insight #2: Own-brand stored value without a visible orchestration layer**
> **Evidence:** Section 4 (Watsons Pay in Taiwan with card-on-file tokenisation and forced 3DS above NT$5,000; Watsons e-Wallet in Malaysia with two dedicated T&C pages) + Section 3B (no orchestrator identified; eLab runs the platform centrally).
> **Pain Point:** Running own float means top-up rails, reconciliation across top-up and spend, refund-to-wallet, and expiry/breakage — per market, on a shared platform, with no routing layer underneath.
> **Yuno Value Proposition:** Top-up acquiring routing, retry logic and unified reconciliation across the providers behind the wallet.
> **Best Success Case:** **Livelo** — loyalty/rewards with a stored-value dynamic, and the decline-recovery mechanism. Say plainly it is Brazil and a pattern match.
> **Outreach Angle:** They built a wallet in Taiwan and another in Malaysia. Two own-brand float products in two markets is a payments engineering commitment.
> **Suggested Subject Line:** *Two wallets, two markets*

> **Insight #3: The IPO puts cost of acceptance on the agenda**
> **Evidence:** Section 6 (dual HK/London listing at ~US$30bn, delayed to 2027, Temasek exiting) + Section 12 (Retail revenue +9% reported but **+5% in local currency**, from the primary filing).
> **Pain Point:** A listing forces margin scrutiny, and headline growth already flatters local-currency performance. Cost of acceptance and approval rate are margin lines that a pre-IPO cost review reaches.
> **Yuno Value Proposition:** Approval-rate uplift and acceptance-cost reduction are among the few payment levers that show up in gross margin rather than as a project cost.
> **Best Success Case:** **Livelo** (recovered revenue) — quantified and linkable.
> **Outreach Angle:** Pre-IPO margin review is the window. Handle with care and never imply inside knowledge — this is public reporting.
> **Suggested Subject Line:** *Acceptance cost before the listing*

> **Insight #4: Failed payments that keep the money and lose the promo**
> **Evidence:** Section 5 (Trustpilot SG 1.9/5 over 119 reviews, verified; "money moves anyway" in PH; **two independent reports that a member discount or voucher is lost when the first payment attempt fails**) + Section 3A (a `queue-api` virtual waiting room on a shared origin, i.e. they manage traffic spikes centrally).
> **Pain Point:** Promotion logic coupled to the first payment attempt means a retry costs the customer their discount. For a retailer with 183m loyalty members whose entire proposition is member pricing, that is a loyalty problem wearing a payments costume.
> **Yuno Value Proposition:** Retry and failover beneath the order, so a second attempt is a payments event rather than a new order that has lost its promo.
> **Best Success Case:** **Livelo** — 50% of failed transactions recovered by routing to a secondary acquirer.
> **Outreach Angle:** Quote one dated review. Never generalise it into "your checkout is broken".
> **Suggested Subject Line:** *When a retry costs the member discount*
