### Orchestration status
**In-house, per-market — and NOT group-wide.** Classified deliberately, not by default.
- **Australia proves an in-house layer exists.** AIA runs its own **`PaymentServicesAPI`** at `https://apiservices.aia.com.au/paymentservicesapi/fingerprint-service-web/api/payment/adhocPaymentUrlGeneration`, behind a central API gateway (`api-gateway.stg.aia.biz:943/gateway/PaymentServicesAPI/1.0/`). - **Singapore proves there is no routing.** One gateway string, `CYBERSOURCE`, hard-coded for cards **and** for PayNow — a single terminal gateway for both the card rails and the domestic A2A rail, with no alternative in the client.
- **Three configurations inside two markets:** AU Priority Protection → AIA's own `api-gateway.*.aia.biz`; AU Tailored Protection → IPSI's simulation host; SG PayEZ → `gateway:"CYBERSOURCE"`. Australia also runs **four separate IVR phone numbers** for telephone payment, one per product line.
- **Global orchestrator: ruled out** on the evidence reached. Zero hits for Spreedly, Primer, Gr4vy, CellPoint, APEXX, Payrails, IXOPAY or Yuno against AIA.
- **Regional orchestrator: ruled out** on the evidence reached. Zero hits for Juspay against AIA Group. ⚠️ The TAL's `Payment Orchestrator` column is **empty** for AIA and says **Juspay** for the separate **Tata AIA Life** row — and that Juspay attribution **could not be confirmed**; 2023 press coverage names **PayU** instead.

## Executive Summary

AIA Group is the largest independent publicly listed pan-Asian life and health insurer, present in **18 markets**, serving **45 million individual policies plus 16 million group-scheme members**, and collecting **US$22.3 billion of renewal premium in the six months to June 2026** — roughly **US$3.7 billion of recurring premium cash per month**. The central payment finding is that **card is a constrained minority rail by AIA's own design, not by accident**: in Hong Kong *"credit cards of other banks can only be used for monthly autopay"*, in Singapore *"AMEX and VISA are accepted for first year premium payment only"* and *"we only accept Singapore issued credit cards"*, in Indonesia only **two banks' virtual accounts** are offered with **no QRIS at all**, and in Thailand the six published channels are ATM, counter, bank-app bill payment, internet banking, bank direct debit and card direct debit. Premium is collected through **bank autopay and direct-debit mandates, virtual accounts, bill-payment biller codes, convenience-store and post-office cash, cheques and bank drafts** — with cards layered on top as a mandate rail rather than a checkout. The infrastructure is **per-market and in-house-fronted, with no group orchestration layer**: Singapore hard-codes a single gateway (`CYBERSOURCE`) for cards *and* PayNow, while Australia runs AIA's own `PaymentServicesAPI` in front of IPSI's EnterpriseSecure — a vendor **Commonwealth Bank acquired in February 2026 and is absorbing through 2026**. The motion is therefore **in-house displacement, market by market**, opening on mandate durability and local-rail coverage rather than on the case for orchestration.

### Section 10: Strategic Insights & Outreach Angles

> **Insight #1: Recurring card is restricted by scheme, by issuing bank and by issuer country — in AIA's own words, on AIA's own pages, in its two largest digital markets.**
> **Evidence:** **Section 4 + Section 3A.** Hong Kong — *"Credit cards of other banks can only be used for monthly autopay"*, with one-off card limited to China Construction Bank (Asia) cards and excluded entirely for Single Premium, Investment-Linked, Loan Repayment, Future Premium Deposit Fund, Top-Up and FlexiMoney ([AIA HK](https://www.aia.com.hk/en/help-and-support/individuals/premium-payment-channels.app.html)). Singapore — *"Use Mastercard for seamless recurring premium payments. AMEX and VISA are accepted for first year premium payment only"* and *"We only accept Singapore issued credit cards"* ([payez2.aia.com.sg](https://payez2.aia.com.sg/recurring-page)), against a single hard-coded `gateway:"CYBERSOURCE"` for every method `[Source Code]`.
> **Pain Point:** A Visa cardholder in Singapore can pay AIA's first-year premium and then **cannot** keep paying by card. A policyholder who moves abroad, or who holds a foreign-issued card, is excluded from Singapore card collection outright. Every one of those customers must be pushed onto a GIRO mandate — and GIRO setup is capped at *"five (5) policies per submission"* and, for non-DBS/POSB banks, requires *"authentication for each policy one at a time"*. Single-scheme recurring acceptance is almost always an acquirer-capability constraint, not a policy preference.
> **Yuno Value Proposition:** Multiple acquirers behind one integration means recurring acceptance stops being limited to whatever one gateway's single acquirer relationship supports. Visa and AMEX recurring become available because a different acquirer in the routing layer supports them. Foreign-issued cards get routed to an acquirer in the cardholder's own geography rather than refused at the door — the corridor argument, per market, not a regional claim.
> **Best Success Case:** **Qatar Airways** — one brand collecting from cardholders across dozens of issuer geographies, where local acquiring per corridor is the mechanism and approval rate is the outcome. **No published figures exist for this relationship; do not attach one.**
> **Outreach Angle:** Your Singapore recurring premium page says Mastercard works for renewals and Visa and AMEX are first-year only, Singapore-issued only. That reads like one acquirer's capability rather than a decision AIA made.
> **Suggested Subject Line:** Mastercard-only on SG renewals

> **Insight #2: Indonesia is the #1 country on the group site and runs on two banks' virtual accounts, with no QRIS.**

### Manual Research Recommendations

> **Area:** **The card-rail share of premium collections** — Section 12, the one number that sizes a Yuno deal
> **Why it matters:** The monthly transaction count is ≥3.75m, but if card is 5% of collections the addressable volume is an order of magnitude smaller than the headline. Every published signal points to card being a minority rail (HK restricts other banks' cards to autopay only; SG recurring is Singapore-issued Mastercard only; ID offers two bank VAs plus marketplaces; TH offers ATM, counter and bank-app bill payment). **Pitching card orchestration without this number risks sizing a deal against premium that never touches a card.**
> **Suggested manual action:** Ask it directly on the first call, per market: *"Of your monthly premium collections in Hong Kong, roughly what share come in on a card versus autopay versus the offline channels?"* It is a question AIA can answer and will not mind answering.

> **Area:** **The authenticated AIA+ payment flow** — Sections 3A, 8 and 9
> **Why it matters:** 14 of 16 markets have no identified PSP purely because the payment step is behind login. The two vendors found (Cybersource SG, IPSI AU) came from unauthenticated surfaces, which means the authenticated surfaces almost certainly expose more.
> **Suggested manual action:** Walk a live AIA+ session with DevTools open in **Hong Kong, Chinese Mainland and Singapore** — the top three by TWPI — and read the network calls on the payment step. If no policy is available, a VPN plus the public PayEZ flow at `payez2.aia.com.sg` will at least confirm the Cybersource handoff and whether 3DS2 is live.

> **Area:** **Chinese Mainland payment channels** — Section 4, a US$7,995m TWPI market with no data
> **Why it matters:** AIA's #2 segment by premium and the report's largest single blind spot. Alipay, WeChat Pay and UnionPay status is entirely unknown, and Mainland domestic acquiring is licence-gated.
> **Suggested manual action:** Have a Mandarin reader walk `aia.com.cn` for a 繳費/缴费/保费支付 channel page, and check the Mainland AIA app's payment options. Do **not** assume Alipay and WeChat Pay are present just because they dominate the market.

> **Area:** **PayTo in Australia, and whether IPSI already offers it** — Section 4, Insight #3
> **Why it matters:** The most checkable gap in the whole account. PayTo is absent from AIA Australia's payment pages while IPSI appears to market a PayTo capability `[UNVERIFIED]`. If AIA's own gateway sells a mandate rail AIA has not switched on, that is a concrete, verifiable observation — and the CommBank integration makes it timely.
> **Suggested manual action:** Verify IPSI's PayTo product at a primary source (ipsi.com.au's own product pages, or the Azupay partnership page). Then confirm whether AIA Australia offers direct debit at all, which the fetched "Making a payment" page did not state.

> **Area:** **Monthly transaction count confirmation** — the mandatory ICP disclosure
> **Why it matters:** The figure is **DERIVED**, with a floor assumption on collection frequency that is mine and not AIA's. The band holds under every sensitivity tested, so it is sound for scoring — but it is not a measurement and is labelled accordingly throughout.
> **Suggested manual action:** Ask for the collection-frequency mix (monthly / quarterly / semi-annual / annual / single premium) per market. AIA's disclosed first-year, renewal and single premium split already hints at it; the frequency mix turns the floor into a real number.

> **Area:** **The procurement unit** — Section 2, and the analyst override note
> **Why it matters:** Payment decisions sit with 17 separately regulated insurance subsidiaries, each with its own regulator and its own policy-admin core (AU on LIFE/400, per the `aia.life400adhoc` config identifier). The group `aia.biz` API gateway and `PaymentServicesAPI` suggest *some* central standard exists. **Whether the buying unit is Group or the market determines the whole forecast.**
> **Suggested manual action:** Establish early whether a Group payments or digital function owns the acceptance standard, or whether each market CFO/COO buys independently. The Sri Lanka × Sampath Bank deal, signed by AIA Insurance Lanka's own Operations managers, suggests the latter — which means **land in one market and expand**, not a group deal.

> **Area:** **Involuntary churn and first-renewal drop-off** — Insight #5
> **Why it matters:** The business case rests on it, and no public number exists. AIA's Thailand persistency ratio of 96.9% is the only adjacent datum found, and persistency is not the same as first-attempt collection success.
> **Suggested manual action:** Ask the §5 discovery questions from `subscription-payments.md`: what share of renewals fail on first attempt, what share recover, is the first-renewal cliff visible in cohort data, are network tokens live anywhere. **Do not assert a benchmark in email.**

> **Area:** **The exposed Australian payment config** — Section 3A
> **Why it matters:** AIA Australia's production payment pages carry a `configJson` meta tag containing a `sharedsecret` UUID and a `userName` ending `.iframe.demo`, pointing at a `simulation.` host. **This is not a sales angle and must not be raised in outreach** — leading with a security observation on a regulated insurer's payment page will end the thread. It is recorded because it is what identified the vendor, and because it suggests the AU payment surface has not been reviewed recently.
> **Suggested manual action:** Note it internally. Do not mention it externally.
