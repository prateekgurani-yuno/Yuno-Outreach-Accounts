> ## 🎯 THE HOOK — an audited disclosure that they pay up to 24% and up to 30% in payment processing, and 1.4% at home
>
> **All five lines below are verbatim from the FY2025 Form 20-F, filed 2026-04-24, and I read every one of them in the filing myself** (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1313310/000162828026026985/grvy-20251231.htm):
>
> | Market | Gravity's own words |
> |---|---|
> | **Korea** (home) | *"We pay a commission for payment processing ranging from **1.4% to 2.8% or Won 200 per transaction**"* |
> | **Taiwan / HK / Macau** | *"We pay a commission for payment processing ranging from **5% to 21% per transaction**"* |
> | **Thailand** | *"We pay a commission for payment processing ranging from **1.5% to 24% per transaction**"* |
> | **Singapore / Malaysia / Philippines** | *"We pay a commission for payment processing per transaction to third-party payment processors"* — **range withheld** |
> | **Latin America** | *"We pay a commission for payment processing ranging from **0.7% to 30% per transaction**"* |
>
> **This is the asymmetry to name, and it sits entirely inside their own stack.** Korea — 6.9% of revenue — is processed at 1.4–2.8%. Taiwan (23.9%), Thailand (9.5%) and LatAm run to 21%, 24% and 30%. They cannot dispute either half; both come from the same filing, on adjacent pages.
>
> ⚠️ **Caveat that must not be skipped.** Total "commission paid" of KRW 326,579m (US$226.1M) in FY2025 **bundles app-store platform fees, royalties and outsourcing**. It is NOT payment-processing cost. **Never present that number as MDR.** The ranges above are the clean disclosure; use only those.
>
> ### The second hook — they published their own currency gap, dated and unsolved
>
> Gravity's LatAm help centre, *"Why am I being charged in USD?"* — **fetched and read by me on 2026-09-20** (https://gravityus.freshdesk.com/en/support/solutions/articles/157000304047-why-am-i-being-charged-in-usd-):
>
> > *"Purchases processed in 'Global' are charged in **USD** (U.S. dollars). We understand that **local currency options are important, and we're currently working to expand our support for more currencies very soon** — so you'll be able to pay in the one that's most convenient for you."*
>
> A merchant that has written *"we're currently working to expand our support for more currencies"* into a public help article has already conceded the problem. There is nothing left to convince them of.

### ⚠️ GATE 2 — App-store trap: **PARTIALLY APPLIES.** Read this before anything else.

The selection thesis said "PC MMO implies web top-up." **That is half wrong.** Audited FY2025 segment split, verified by me in the 20-F:

| FY2025 | KRW m | % of total |
|---|---|---|
| Online (PC) games | 90,339 | **16.1%** |
| Mobile games | 455,235 | **81.2%** |
| Other | 14,974 | 2.7% |
| **Total** | **560,548** | **US$388.0M** |

**Four things keep the account qualified anyway, all sourced:**

**1. The web-billed floor alone is large and growing.** Online-games *micro-transaction* revenue — billed through Gravity's own web PSPs, no app store involved — was **KRW 76,875m (13.7% of group) in FY2025, up from 61,425m**. In Q2 2026 the online segment grew **+35.0% YoY while total revenue fell 5.2%** (6-K filed 2026-08-07). The mix is shifting *toward* the part orchestration can touch.

**2. They run their own web top-up storefronts for their MOBILE titles.** `topup-mobile.gnjoy.in.th` is operated by Gravity Game Tech and advertises packs *"only through the GNJOY Mobile Top-up channel"*; `gnjoy.asia/topup/roia/` serves web top-up for Ragnarok Origin, Ragnarok X, Ragnarok Crush, RO Idle Adventure Plus, Landverse and Ragnarok: Go — all mobile titles. Exclusive-pack incentives on a web channel are a deliberate migration off IAP.

**3. The Brazil proof point.** In Brazil, **web-billed PC micro-transaction revenue grew 6.1× (KRW 2,072m → 12,571m)** while app-store-billed mobile revenue roughly halved. Both figures from the same 20-F table, verified by me.

**4. They say the margin reason out loud:** *"profits generated from our mobile games… are generally lower than our profits generated from online games. This is because in order to gain access to our games on mobile app stores, **the primary distribution channel for our mobile games**, we must enter into revenue-sharing arrangements that result in lower profit margins."* Gross margin fell to 35.0% (2025) from 38.7% (2024) *"due to higher platform service fees."*

❌ **NOT ESTABLISHED, and it is the one number that would settle this outright:** the 20-F **does not disaggregate mobile revenue between IAP and web top-up.** The web share of that 81.2% is unknown. `[INFERENCE, not confirmed]` — the exclusive-pack incentives and the Brazil pattern point to a deliberate migration, but there is no figure. **Do not let an email imply a split.**
