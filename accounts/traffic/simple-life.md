## Read — three things changed, and they matter

### 1. The growth story reversed
In Snapshot 1 (Jun–Aug) the core was **growing hard**: US ▲36.13%, Brazil ▲71.14%, France ▲203.52%,
Spain ▲161.27%, Italy ▲72.33%. In Snapshot 2 (Jul–Sep) **every one of those is falling**, several
steeply: US ▼27.51%, Brazil ▼43.08%, France ▼43.78%, Spain ▼54.32%, Italy ▼43.67%.

**Do not read this as decline without testing seasonality first.** Diet and fasting apps have a
pronounced January peak and a northern-hemisphere autumn trough, and the two windows overlap by two
months, so this may be a seasonal roll-off rather than a business problem. ⚠️ **Flagged for
research, not concluded.**

### 2. Only four markets in the visible table are growing — and two are APAC
| Market | Change |
|---|---|
| 🇿🇦 South Africa | ▲ 98.51% |
| 🇯🇵 **Japan** | ▲ **38.48%** |
| 🇦🇺 **Australia** | ▲ **16.81%** |

**Japan and Australia are the only two APAC markets on the board, and both are rising while the
core falls.** That asymmetry is the entire reason this account is being revisited.

### 3. The engagement data is new, and Japan tops it
Snapshot 1 gave engagement only as a range. Snapshot 2 is per-country, and:
- **Japan has the highest pages/visit in the entire table — 18.56**, against a 12.43 US average.
- **Japan's audience share (3.69%) EXCEEDS its traffic share (2.75%)** — more unique users than
  visit-volume alone implies.
- **Australia's audience share (1.02%) likewise exceeds its traffic share (0.77%)**, and its
  **36.03% bounce rate is the second-lowest in the table**, behind only the US.
- Contrast France (00:28, 3.19 pages, 60.84% bounce) and Türkiye (00:25, 1.55 pages, 70.32% bounce)
  — those look like paid-traffic bounces, not engaged funnels.

**Japanese and Australian users engage harder than anyone except Americans.**
