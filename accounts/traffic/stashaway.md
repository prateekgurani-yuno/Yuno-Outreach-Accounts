## ⚠️ Analyst notes — read before using this table

**1. Singapore is 39.82% — comfortably BELOW the 60% threshold**, so the ICP row *"High traffic
outside home"* **fires (+2)**. Outside-Singapore traffic is 60.18%.

**2. The top three markets are exactly their licensed markets.** Singapore, Malaysia and Hong
Kong together are **71.96%** of all traffic. Everything else is a long tail. Any rail analysis
that does not start with **PayNow/FAST (SG)**, **FPX/DuitNow (MY)** and **FPS (HK)** is looking
in the wrong place.

**3. 🚩 UAE has collapsed: ↓85.69%**, the largest single move in the table, while still holding
4.71% share. **This needs explaining before anything is written about MENA.** A drop that size
usually means a market exit, a licence change, a domain migration or a redirect — all of which
are findings in their own right. Do not describe the UAE as a growth market.

**4. 🚩 Hong Kong ↑210.32% and Indonesia ↑503.32%.** Hong Kong is plausible as a genuine push
(it is a licensed market and already 7.60%). **Indonesia at 1.77% is NOT a licensed market on
the TAL's own description**, so a 503% jump there needs a cause before it is treated as demand.

**5. ⚠️ Engagement is LOW almost everywhere, and that is expected here — but it caps what the
traffic can tell us.** Singapore 00:00:54 / 1.97 pages, Malaysia 00:01:11, Thailand 00:00:30,
India 00:00:37, **Korea 00:00:15 / 1.34 pages**. For a wealth manager this is normal: the
marketing site is a thin front door and all real activity happens **inside the logged-in app**,
which SimilarWeb does not see. **Consequence: these visit counts are a weak proxy for deposit
volume and must not be used to derive transaction counts.**
Two rows are anomalous and should be treated with suspicion rather than excitement:
**UAE 00:07:26 but only 1.40 pages and 59.14% bounce**, and **Indonesia 00:12:15 with 2.10
pages and a 24.04% bounce** — long dwell with almost no depth is not a normal browsing pattern.

**6. ⚠️ The TAL says the website is `stashaway.com`; this data is for `stashaway.sg` + 4 unnamed
domains.** That strongly suggests a **per-market ccTLD structure** (plausibly `.sg`, `.com.my`,
`.hk`, `.ae`, `.co.th`), which matters: a per-country domain usually means a per-country entity,
a per-country funding rail and a per-country acquiring relationship. **Resolve all five domains
and check each for redirects** — a domain that 301s elsewhere is not a separate property and its
traffic is an artefact.

## 🛑 THE FRAMING THAT DECIDES THIS ACCOUNT

**StashAway is a licensed wealth manager, not a merchant selling goods.** Its "payment" is a
**customer depositing money into an investment account**, and in these three markets that
overwhelmingly means **bank transfer, not cards**: PayNow/FAST in Singapore, FPX/DuitNow in
Malaysia, FPS in Hong Kong. Many investment platforms deliberately **do not accept cards for
funding at all**, because card-funded investing carries AML, chargeback and cost-of-acceptance
problems.

**This must be established before the pitch is written, exactly as the app-store split is for a
consumer subscription business.** If deposits are bank-transfer-only and card volume is
negligible, card-oriented orchestration has limited relevance and the account may warrant an
**analyst override**. If instead they take cards for funding, or run **recurring deposits**
(standing instructions, GIRO, direct debit, autopay), that recurring rail is the real
conversation. Either outcome is a legitimate finding; assuming the first is not.
