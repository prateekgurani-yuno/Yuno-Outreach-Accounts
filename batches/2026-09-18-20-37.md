**1. 🔴 Fly91 failed the volume gate on its own published number, and the target list's
orchestrator entry was refuted.**
Fly91's own press index states **"Over 4.8 Lakh Passengers"** across its first 24 months —
**~20,000 a month**, half the 40,000 floor *before* any divisor. Both divisors then push it
lower: multiple passengers per PNR on a leisure-heavy network, and a confirmed agent/OTA
channel (a live partner booking portal, a Paytm Travel inventory tie-up, MakeMyTrip).
Separately, `accounts/apac-tal.csv` carried **`Payment Orchestrator: Juspay`** — **refuted**,
with zero Juspay footprint anywhere on Fly91's properties and Fly91 absent from Juspay's own
airlines page. **The TAL row is corrected in place** rather than blanked, so it is not
silently re-added. **Diarised for revisit in 2028** — the 40-aircraft ATR order of 3 Sept 2026
is a 7–10× capacity increase and the only path to clearing the bar.

**2. ⚠️ Fever is a COMPETITIVE motion, not greenfield — and the stub was wrong twice.**
Their production config, verified byte-for-byte on two APAC city pages, shows a **global
orchestration layer live above Nuvei, Checkout.com, PayPal and Google Pay**. The
`/full-outreach` rule permits a competitive motion only where research surfaces a concrete
gap. **It did, cleanly: orchestration in production and zero local rails across nine live
APAC markets.** The sharpest observation in either batch is that **Fever ships
fully-configured Kakao and Naver OAuth login for Korea and has no Korean payment rail at
all** — zero occurrences of KakaoPay, Naver Pay, Toss or PAYCO. Also: **the entity is Fever
Labs Inc. of Delaware/New York, not Madrid.** Both corrections are in the file; writing
"Madrid-based" in email one would have been an avoidable own goal.

**3. 🚩 A false positive nearly inverted the HK Express answer, and the pattern is new.**
The first research pass returned **28 hits for `adyen-checkout` and `checkoutshopper-live.adyen.com`**
in what appeared to be HK Express assets. They were **stale scratchpad files left by a
different task** — filenames like `pages_creator-join` belong to another company's site
entirely. Re-running against only freshly-fetched assets gives **zero Adyen**. **Add to the
running false-positive list: stale scratchpad artifacts are as dangerous as substring
collisions.** Always re-fetch before asserting a vendor. (Also logged this batch: an Expedia
UI component named `...-stripe.js` that is CSS table-striping, not Stripe.)

**11. 🔴 iTicket failed the same gate, and its own traffic number is what settled it.**
Verified first-hand on `hello.iticket.co.nz`: **10M+ tickets sold, 3k+ partners, 185k+ monthly
website visitors, 20+ years**. Clearing 40,000 orders/month would need **more than 21.6% of
every unique monthly visitor to complete a paid order** — not credible. Three independent
derivations (traffic, lifetime volume, event count) land between ~8k and ~25k. Cross-border
surface is near nil too: NZ and Australia run as **two separate domestic entities on separate
domains in separate currencies**.
📌 **But keep the file.** iTicket runs a **dual merchant-of-record model** — per-transaction
routing between its own merchant account and the promoter's, with different refund liability
on each side and a visual distinction at checkout. **That is the most interesting payment
architecture found in either batch, and it belongs to the smallest company in either batch.**
If iTicket is ever acquired into a larger ANZ ticketing group, the account becomes relevant
immediately.

**12. 🔑 A technique worth reusing: when the consumer site is bot-walled, try the B2B site.**
`www.iticket.co.nz` returns **HTTP 429** (Vercel challenge) to every user agent including
Googlebot, with no Wayback fallback. **`hello.iticket.co.nz` — their promoter-facing site — is
wide open and carries the volume figures, the exhaustive payment-method list and the
settlement terms.** That split is what made the account researchable at all.
