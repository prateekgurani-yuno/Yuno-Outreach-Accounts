### 🔑 The single biggest finding is segment-wide, not per-account
**Three of the four hotel groups run on Sabre SynXis, and in every case the card does not settle centrally.**

| Group | Where the card settles | Evidence (orchestrator-verified) |
|---|---|---|
| **Langham** | **5 of 33 properties have a gateway at all.** 28 return empty `paymentGateway` | Live availability API: Sydney/Melbourne/Auckland `cybersource`, Gold Coast/Toronto `adyen`, rest `""` |
| **Mandarin Oriental** | **192 gateway flags across 48 properties, every one `false`** | Their own embedded production config, HTML-decoded and counted |
| **Peninsula** | Each property is a **separate SynXis hotel, single currency, `chainID` empty** | Tokyo JPY-only, Hong Kong HKD-only |
| **Rosewood** | Same pattern; London's non-refundable rate promises **a phone call to arrange prepayment** | 14 booking-policy codes across 12 properties |

**This is one architecture, not four problems.** Guest books on brand.com → card is tokenised or guaranteed centrally → **the property bills it at check-out through its own PMS and its own acquirer.** Consequences:
1. **The orchestratable volume may be near zero** at all four. Group-wide folio counts are large and almost entirely card-present at property POS, which orchestration cannot touch.
2. **The buyer is not at group HQ.** Merchant of record is frequently the third-party owner's operating company — at Mandarin Oriental only 12 of 45 hotels are owned at all.
3. 🚩 **Sabre already sells the answer.** **SynXis Pay, powered by CellPoint Digital orchestration, launched 3 Mar 2025 — native to the engine three of these four already license.** That is the competitive path of least resistance and it is pre-integrated.
4. **Rail-gap framing is the wrong pitch here.** You cannot take a card *guarantee* on a QRIS QR, a PromptPay transfer or an Alipay redirect. The honest arguments are **corridor breadth** and **the handful of properties that do take money at booking** (Mandarin Oriental: Beijing-Qianmen, Kuala Lumpur, Bangkok suites, Lucerne — 4 of 48).

**Recommendation: do not run four parallel sequences.** Pick the one with a central prepay surface and treat the rest as one segment play. **Langham is already overridden to 🔴 — one exploratory touch, not twelve.**

### 🚩 New environment traps — four of them, all silent failures
Every one returns a plausible **zero** rather than an error. Added to the running list alongside `grep -P` and stale scratchpad assets:
1. **CloudFront serves Brotli.** Plain `curl` stores compressed bytes; every vendor grep returns **0**. **Use `curl --compressed`.** This nearly inverted the SpinX verdict.
2. **Client-rendered pages.** Stripping scripts from FWD's Hong Kong premiums page leaves **61 characters** — just the title. All eight payment methods live in `__NEXT_DATA__`. A naive extraction would have reported *"no payment methods"* on the most important page in that report. **Grep the raw source, never extracted text.**
3. **HTML-entity-encoded config.** Mandarin Oriental's property config is **double-encoded** (`&quot;key&quot;:false`). A grep for `key":false` returns **0**; the bare key returns **48**. **`html.unescape()` twice, then match.** I nearly filed their headline as unreproducible.
4. **Malformed `content-encoding: utf-8`** on SpinX's gateway bundle makes `curl --compressed` fail outright.

**Also: `pdftotext` and `pdftoppm` are not installed.** Read PDFs by decompressing FlateDecode streams in Python — that is how OneDegree's statutory GWP and Netmarble's per-title revenue were obtained.
